HeartGlance

Your latest heart rate and how old it is, right on your Apple Watch face. A small, watch-only app for watchOS 26 or later. Coming to the App Store.

Questions or bugs? Write to screamcode@gmail.com. Include the app version, watchOS version and what happened. Please omit heart-rate values and other health information from screenshots unless you want to share them.

Frequently asked

Does it measure my heart rate?

No. HeartGlance reads the latest heart-rate sample already in Apple Health. It never starts a workout, takes extra measurements or writes to Health.

How do I add it to my watch face?

Touch and hold your watch face, tap Edit, choose a complication slot and select HeartGlance. Circular, rectangular, inline and corner slots are supported.

Why is the number old or grey?

Apple controls background measurements and complication refreshes. HeartGlance shows the sample's real age, and dims readings older than 30 minutes. Opening the app refreshes from Health, but does not take a new measurement. It is not a live monitor.

Why does it say “No Heart Rate”?

There may be no reading available, or Health access may be off. Check HeartGlance's Heart Rate permission in Health settings on the watch or paired iPhone, wear and unlock your watch, then reopen the app. Apple does not tell apps whether read permission was denied.

What happens while my watch is locked?

HealthKit may be unavailable, so the complication can use its cached reading. Its BPM is marked privacy-sensitive. Review your watch's Always On settings if you do not want complication data visible when your wrist is lowered.

How do I remove my data?

Turn off HeartGlance's Heart Rate permission and reopen the app to clear the cached reading once Health returns no samples. Diagnostics → Reset Counters clears diagnostic records. Delete HeartGlance to remove its local app data; it does not delete Apple Health records. See the privacy policy for retention and backup details.

Health and safety

HeartGlance is for general information. It is not a medical device, diagnosis tool, continuous monitor or emergency alert service. Do not use an old reading to decide whether you need medical care.
