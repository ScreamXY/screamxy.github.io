Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 October 2026

PulseGlance displays existing heart-rate readings on Apple Watch, made by Pascal Schrei. The app was called HeartGlance until 15 October 2026.

Who is responsible

Pascal Schrei, Switzerland. Contact: screamcode@gmail.com. See the legal notice.

Summary

PulseGlance does not upload your health data or collect it on a developer server. There are no accounts, analytics, advertisements, tracking or third-party SDKs.

Health access and purpose

With your permission, the app and complication read heart-rate samples from Apple Health to show the newest valid value, its measurement time and its age. A sample's source (the device or app that recorded it) is also available in diagnostics. PulseGlance requests read access only to heart rate. It never writes Health data, starts a workout or requests additional heart-rate measurements.

Local storage and retention

The app and its complication share an App Group container on your watch. It holds one latest reading (BPM, time and source), permission-request state, and limited diagnostics: query times, counters, the last errors, and up to 20 recent timeline generations and background deliveries. Diagnostics do not keep a history of heart-rate values. A newer reading replaces the cached reading; it has no automatic age-based expiry.

PulseGlance does not implement cloud sync. Local app data may be included in watch backups managed by Apple and your device settings. Apple Health's own storage, syncing and backup are separate from PulseGlance.

Revoke access and delete data

You can turn off PulseGlance's Heart Rate permission in Health settings on your watch or paired iPhone. Reopen PulseGlance afterwards: when Health returns no samples, the app clears the cached reading and requests a complication refresh. Revoking permission alone does not instantly erase an already displayed complication; watchOS controls refreshes. If Health is locked or a query fails, the cached reading can remain available.

Diagnostics → Reset Counters clears the app's diagnostic record and invalidates the complication's old diagnostic record; the extension replaces that record on a later write. The reading remains. Delete the app to remove its local app data. Existing device backups are managed separately. PulseGlance never deletes the original Apple Health records.

Security and sharing

HealthKit permission and the watchOS app sandbox restrict access. The shared container is used by PulseGlance and its complication. BPM in the complication is marked privacy-sensitive; visibility also depends on your lock and Always On settings. PulseGlance does not send Health data to the developer, advertisers or other services.

Support and websites

If you choose to email support, your email address and anything you include are processed through Gmail to answer your request. Please avoid sending health data. Correspondence is kept while handling your request and can be deleted on request unless retention is legally required. Opening support or privacy links loads the public website; its separate hosting privacy notice applies.

Your rights

You can ask what data I hold about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted. Write to the address above. I cannot access or recover your local Health data.
